# Review scope and inclusion principles
## Scope
The review examines how acute ischemic and hemorrhagic brain injury relates to communication phenotypes and recovery, with emphasis on the first 90 days. It links five connected domains:
1. **Vascular and tissue injury:** infarct core, penumbra, reperfusion, no-reflow, hematoma, perihematomal edema, blood–brain barrier disruption, and secondary injury.
2. **Neural substrates:** lesion topography, white-matter integrity, tract disconnection, diaschisis, distributed language/speech networks, and network reorganization.
3. **Communication phenotypes:** aphasia, dysarthria, apraxia of speech, and cognitive-communication impairment.
4. **Clinical measurement:** neurological severity, lesion/perfusion measures, impairment-level language/speech tests, functional communication, quality of life, and participation.
5. **Rehabilitation and recovery:** restorative language/speech therapy, communication-partner approaches, compensatory supports, AAC, and transfer to daily communication.
## Inclusion principles
- **Acute-stage relevance:** prioritize studies and resources that inform acute stroke or the first 90 days after stroke.
- **Mechanistic relevance:** include ischemic/hemorrhagic injury, perfusion, reperfusion, blood–brain barrier, edema, inflammation, lesion–symptom mapping, white-matter disconnection, and neural-network studies when they inform communication outcomes.
- **Communication specificity:** prioritize aphasia, dysarthria, apraxia of speech, cognitive-communication, connected speech, functional communication, and participation outcomes.
- **Clinical utility:** include guidelines, prospective cohorts, randomized trials, validated measures, resource descriptors, and individual-participant-data initiatives when they inform assessment or rehabilitation.
- **Chronic-stage evidence:** chronic aphasia studies are retained to interpret persistent network organization, outcome measurement, or intervention mechanisms; they are not treated as direct evidence of acute-stage recovery.
- **Resource eligibility:** data resources are compared by population, sampling unit, stroke stage, modality, communication outcome coverage, follow-up, access conditions, and known/unresolved overlap.
- **Evidence boundaries:** dataset records, participants, imaging sessions, transcripts, contributing studies, protocol targets, and validation samples are kept distinct. Their counts are not added as if they represented independent participants.
## Literature identification
Relevant literature was identified through searches of major biomedical databases and scholarly platforms, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Google Scholar, and SpringerLink, supplemented by reference-list screening of relevant reviews, clinical guidelines, and original studies. Major clinical trial registries and primary resource repositories were consulted where relevant to verify trial status and the characteristics of selected cohorts and data resources.
This is a focused narrative synthesis with a structured evidence map, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No pooled effect estimates are calculated and no PRISMA flow diagram is claimed. Search concepts cover acute ischemic stroke, intracerebral hemorrhage, aphasia, dysarthria, apraxia of speech, cognitive-communication disorders, perfusion/reperfusion, lesion and network disruption, functional communication, and neurorehabilitation. The current manuscript files do not contain database-by-database exact Boolean strings or a complete screening log; these should not be represented as available unless separately documented by the authors.

# Conceptual framework
## Injury-to-recovery pathway
```text
Acute vascular event
        |
        +-------------------------------+
        |                               |
  Ischemic stroke                 Intracerebral hemorrhage
        |                               |
  Occlusion / perfusion            Hematoma / mass effect
  core–penumbra                    primary tissue disruption
        |                               |
  Reperfusion / no-reflow          Thrombin / heme toxicity
  microvascular injury             BBB injury / edema / inflammation
        |                               |
        +---------------+---------------+
                        |
              Tissue and network effects
       lesion location / tract disconnection / diaschisis
            residual connectivity / reorganization
                        |
              Communication phenotypes
       aphasia / dysarthria / apraxia / cognitive-communication
                        |
             Repeated multidomain assessment
       impairment → activity → functional communication → participation
                        |
               Rehabilitation and support
       restorative therapy + partner strategies + AAC/compensation
                        |
                Meaningful recovery outcomes
```
## Central scientific questions
1. How do ischemic and hemorrhagic injury processes produce different but partly convergent disruptions of communication-related networks?
2. Which lesion, perfusion, white-matter, and network features explain heterogeneity in aphasia, dysarthria, and cognitive-communication phenotypes beyond global stroke severity?
3. How do tissue injury, reperfusion, secondary injury, and network reorganization relate to domain-specific communication change during the first 90 days?
4. Can existing cohorts link acute imaging and treatment response to repeated communication-specific outcomes and rehabilitation exposure?
5. Which measures distinguish impairment change from everyday communication, communication access, and participation?

# Selected clinical cohorts and data resources
Eleven resources are compared as complementary evidence sources, not as one pooled cohort. Sample figures below preserve the unit reported by the source. Current access terms must be checked at the provider site before reuse.
## 1. SOOP
- **Sample frame:** 1,715 total; 1,461 acute ischemic stroke; 1,106 survivors with demographic/impairment data; WAB findings in a subset.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** MRI; clinical variables; NIHSS/mRS; partial WAB.
- **Access/version:** OpenNeuro ds004889 v1.1.2; CC0 1.0 data release.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Functional communication is not systematically measured; WAB coverage is a subset.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004889.v1.1.2](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004889.v1.1.2)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Absher2024SOOP; SOOPOpenNeuro2024`
## 2. Johns Hopkins acute-stroke MRI
- **Sample frame:** 2,888 acute/early-subacute MRI cases, 2009–2019.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Clinical MRI; expert DWI masks; NIHSS; 90-day mRS for a subset.
- **Access/version:** ICPSR 38464, Version 6; restricted-use data.
- **Interpretive limitation:** No systematic language or communication profile; access restrictions apply.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.3886/ICPSR38464.v6](https://doi.org/10.3886/ICPSR38464.v6)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Liu2023JHU; JHUICPSR2025`
## 3. ISLES’22
- **Sample frame:** 400 multicentre MRI cases; 250 public training and 150 hidden test cases.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** DWI/ADC/FLAIR; expert lesion masks.
- **Access/version:** Zenodo; public training split under CC BY 4.0.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Segmentation benchmark, not a clinical outcome cohort; no functional communication outcomes.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7153326](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7153326)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `HernandezPetzsche2022ISLES; ISLES22Zenodo2022`
## 4. ISLES’24
- **Sample frame:** 245 successfully reperfused LVO patients; 149 public and 96 hidden cases.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Hyperacute CT within 24 h; post-interventional MRI at 2–9 days; 3-month NIHSS/mRS.
- **Access/version:** Zenodo; public training split under CC BY-SA-NC 4.0.
- **Interpretive limitation:** 46 cases overlap with ISLES’22; infarct masks were initialized using an ISLES’22-derived model before expert correction; no communication-specific outcomes.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16731717](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16731717)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Riedel2026ISLES; ISLES24Zenodo2026`
## 5. Aphasia Recovery Cohort (ARC)
- **Sample frame:** 230 chronic post-stroke aphasia participants; 902 imaging sessions; mean imaging time 1,638 days after stroke.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Structural/diffusion/fMRI; behavioural testing; WAB-AQ.
- **Access/version:** OpenNeuro ds004884 v1.0.2; CC0 release.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Chronic-stage resource, not a first-90-day cohort; participant-level overlap with SOOP remains unverified.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004884.v1.0.2](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004884.v1.0.2)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Gibson2024ARC; ARCOpenNeuro2024`
## 6. PLORAS
- **Sample frame:** 750 participants in a January 2015 snapshot; time since stroke ranged from weeks to 40 years.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Structural and functional imaging; standardized language/cognitive measures including CAT; follow-up.
- **Access/version:** Repository access conditions should be checked with the original resource.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Historical participant count; broad post-stroke interval; PLORAS speech contributes to SONIVA.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.03.083](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.03.083)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Seghier2016PLORAS`
## 7. SONIVA
- **Sample frame:** 618 stroke participants and 189 controls; 26,344 transcripts; longitudinal recordings from >100 participants.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Speech/audio and transcripts; single-word naming, reading, repetition, picture description.
- **Access/version:** Imperial College Helix fixed release; transcript/metadata access differs from controlled audio access.
- **Interpretive limitation:** 25,429 single-word records versus 915 picture descriptions; single-word tasks comprise 96.5% of transcripts; counts are transcripts, not participants; incorporates PLORAS- and IC3-derived material.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.82186/ra9dv-4az59](https://doi.org/10.82186/ra9dv-4az59)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Sanguedolce2026SONIVA; SONIVAHelix2026`
## 8. AphasiaBank
- **Sample frame:** Original 2011 report: 145 people with aphasia and 126 controls from 12 sites.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Video-linked discourse; CHAT transcripts; standardized elicitation tasks.
- **Access/version:** TalkBank tiered data-access levels.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Original report counts are not current corpus totals; mixed stages and elicited tasks limit inference about acute everyday communication.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://talkbank.org/aphasia/access.html](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/access.html)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `MacWhinney2011AphasiaBank; AphasiaBankAccess2026`
## 9. RELEASE
- **Sample frame:** 5,928 individual-participant records from 174 datasets; 28 countries and 23 languages.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** 64 language measures; therapy variables; individual-participant data.
- **Access/version:** Access varies by contributing dataset and data-use terms.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Functional communication measures in 1,591 participants from 29/174 datasets (16.7%); median recruitment 321 days post-stroke (IQR 30–1,156); no common imaging layer.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.1080/02687038.2021.1897081](https://doi.org/10.1080/02687038.2021.1897081)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Williams2022RELEASE`
## 10. IC3
- **Sample frame:** Protocol target 300; validation sample 90 stroke survivors and >6,000 controls; registry estimated enrollment 700.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** Repeated cognitive tasks; MRI/biomarker substudies; 18 cognitive and 4 optional speech-production tasks.
- **Access/version:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05885295; registry status and enrollment estimates are not achieved recruitment.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Single-site prospective platform; optional speech tasks do not form a comprehensive communication battery and do not measure conversation/participation; IC3 speech contributes to SONIVA.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05885295](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05885295)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Gruia2023IC3; Gruia2025IC3Validation; IC3Registry2026`
## 11. ERICH
- **Sample frame:** Approximately 3,000 enrolled; 2,942 subjects in versioned imaging release.
- **Data modalities/outcomes:** ICH CT/MR imaging; hematoma and global outcomes; 1,472 MR sessions in versioned release.
- **Access/version:** Controlled access via Data@Becker; iCDKP portal provides project-level information.
- **Interpretive limitation:** Portal display reports 1,452 MR sessions versus 1,472 in versioned release; these are sessions, not participants; no communication-specific trajectory.
- **Primary source/resource:** [https://doi.org/10.17632/z33hf3d4x3.1](https://doi.org/10.17632/z33hf3d4x3.1)
- **Manuscript citation keys:** `Woo2013ERICH; ERICHData2026; ERICHPortal2026`
## Cross-resource provenance and overlap notes
- **ISLES'22 / ISLES'24:** 46 cases overlap. ISLES'24 infarct masks were initialized with an ISLES'22-derived model before expert correction; cross-release model evaluation should account for both participant overlap and label-generation dependence.
- **SOOP / ARC:** companion resources; participant-level overlap has not been established in the current evidence map and should not be assumed absent.
- **PLORAS / SONIVA:** PLORAS speech material contributes to SONIVA; resource-level counts should not be treated as independent participant totals.
- **IC3 / SONIVA:** IC3 speech material also contributes to SONIVA; distinguish the prospective protocol, validation sample, registry estimate, and speech-resource records.
- **RELEASE:** an individual-participant-data synthesis across contributing datasets, not a new prospective cohort; contributing-study structure and repeated participants must be considered before any re-analysis.
- **ERICH:** distinguish enrolled participants, released subjects, and MR sessions. The versioned dataset reports 1,472 MR sessions, whereas the portal display reports 1,452; sessions are not participants.

# Clinical assessment and outcome-measure index
| Domain | Measure / source | Main construct | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global neurological severity | NIHSS; acute stroke guidelines | Neurological deficit severity | Language/speech items do not provide a full communication profile. |
| Global disability | mRS | Overall disability | Does not isolate language, intelligibility, conversation, or participation. |
| Acute aphasia screen | LAST | Rapid aphasia screening | Screening is not a comprehensive language profile. |
| Multidomain language | QAB; WAB-R; CAT | Language impairment across domains | Structured task performance does not establish daily communication; coverage differs by tool. |
| Dysarthria | FDA-II; COAST; TOM and other measures | Speech-motor impairment and related outcomes | Psychometric evidence and domain coverage remain uneven. |
| Functional communication | COAST; discourse measures; functional communication frameworks | Communication in context and interaction | Context, modality, partner, and elicitation influence scores. |
| Participation / quality of life | SAQOL-39; ROMA outcome framework | Communication-related life impact and treatment outcomes | Quality of life and participation are not equivalent to direct conversational performance. |
| AAC / communication access | Low-technology AAC and partner-supported communication | Compensatory communication access | Access and participation gains should not be mislabelled as restoration of the damaged language network. |

# Rehabilitation and clinical-trial evidence
The intervention literature is heterogeneous in stroke stage, treatment target, dose, comparator, and outcome. Acute/subacute trials should not be pooled conceptually with chronic aphasia studies without explicit qualification.
| Study | Population / stage | Intervention contrast or focus | Interpretation for this review |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACT NoW (`Bowen2012EnhancedCommunication`) | 170 participants with aphasia and/or dysarthria within two weeks of stroke | Enhanced therapist-led communication therapy versus attention-matched social contact | Both groups improved; the six-month between-group difference on the Therapy Outcome Measure was not significant. This tests added benefit over an active comparator, not whether rehabilitation is ineffective. |
| VERSE (`Godecke2021VERSE`) | 246 people with acute post-stroke aphasia | Higher-intensity very early aphasia therapy versus usual care | Higher intensity did not outperform usual care at 12 weeks. |
| Big CACTUS (`Palmer2019BigCACTUS`) | 278 people with chronic aphasia | Self-managed computerized word-retrieval practice versus usual care/attention control | Trained word retrieval improved; transfer to conversation and communication-related quality of life was not established. |
| COMPARE (`Rose2022COMPARE`) | 201 people with chronic aphasia | CIAT-Plus or multimodality aphasia therapy versus usual care | Domain-specific benefits should be distinguished from the primary aphasia-severity endpoint and discourse transfer. |
| Low-technology AAC RCT (`Huang2025LowTechAAC`) | 12 participants; hospital-based post-stroke aphasia | AAC plus regular aphasia treatment versus treatment alone | Preliminary, small-sample evidence; not sufficient for firm efficacy conclusions. |
| PAIL-RcA (`Dietz2025PAILRcA`) | Chronic aphasia; treatment construct/protocol resource | AAC-induced language recovery framework | Protocol/theoretical resource; not equivalent to a completed efficacy trial. |
| Conversation-group RCT (`Hoover2025ConversationRCT`) | Chronic aphasia | Conversation group treatment | Supports evaluation of language and communication outcomes beyond impairment tests. |
| LEXI (`Wischmann2025LEXI`) | Acute post-stroke aphasia | Tablet-assisted speech/language therapy | Acute-stage digital rehabilitation evidence; interpret with its trial design and endpoints. |
| Digital dysarthria trial (`Kim2026DigitalDysarthria`) | Post-stroke dysarthria | Digital speech therapy noninferiority trial | Relevant to speech-motor treatment; not interchangeable with aphasia outcomes. |
| Intensive language-action therapy (`Pulvermuller2026ILAT`) | Chronic post-stroke aphasia | Communication-based intensive language therapy | Informs treatment mechanisms and communication practice; not direct proof of first-90-day efficacy. |
| LEXI brain-frailty analysis (`Wischmann2026BrainFrailtyLEXI`) | Post-hoc analysis of the LEXI RCT | Brain frailty and language recovery | Prognostic/post-hoc evidence; distinguish from a separately randomized intervention. |

## Trial interpretation rules
- Report onset-to-treatment interval, dose, adherence, target domain, comparator, and follow-up whenever available.
- Separate acute/subacute from chronic-stage populations.
- Distinguish trained-item improvement from generalization to connected speech, everyday communication, and participation.
- Distinguish restorative language/speech treatment from compensatory AAC and partner-supported communication.
- Do not infer that a non-significant between-group difference means no within-group improvement or no value of usual care.

# Research gaps and priorities
## Current evidence gaps
1. **Temporal mismatch:** acute imaging resources capture hours or days, while ARC is chronic, PLORAS spans weeks to decades, and RELEASE has a median recruitment time of 321 days. These sources do not form a common onset-aligned trajectory.
2. **Outcome mismatch:** imaging cohorts emphasize lesion, perfusion, NIHSS, and mRS; language repositories emphasize impairment-level or elicited speech; functional communication and participation are less consistently measured.
3. **Linkage mismatch:** acute imaging, reperfusion, speech/language phenotyping, rehabilitation exposure, and patient-reported outcomes are rarely available for the same participants at repeated time points.
4. **Phenotype imbalance:** aphasia is better represented than dysarthria, apraxia of speech, cognitive-communication impairment, and multimodal communication support in early stroke resources.
5. **Sampling and provenance:** participant counts, sessions, transcripts, datasets, registry targets, and validation samples are often conflated. Known and unresolved overlap can compromise cross-resource validation if not tracked.
6. **Transferability:** chronic aphasia studies inform persistent network organization and treatment mechanisms, but should not be treated as direct evidence for acute recovery or first-90-day treatment effects.
7. **Clinical translation:** imaging/network markers are not clinically meaningful communication outcomes unless their predictive value is validated against language, speech, functional communication, and participation.
## Future research priorities
- Establish prospective, onset-aligned cohorts linking acute CT/MRI, perfusion, reperfusion and secondary injury markers to repeated communication assessments.
- Harmonize separate measures for aphasia, dysarthria, apraxia of speech, cognitive-communication, functional communication, and participation; do not collapse them into a single undifferentiated communication score.
- Record treatment timing, dose, adherence, rehabilitation exposure, communication-partner support, and AAC use alongside biological and behavioural outcomes.
- Validate lesion, white-matter, connectome, and perfusion markers against meaningful communication outcomes and across independent cohorts.
- Improve representation of ICH and non-aphasic communication disorders in acute-stage cohorts.
- Publish versioned data dictionaries, sampling-unit definitions, participant-overlap statements, train/test provenance, access conditions, and code/analysis provenance.
